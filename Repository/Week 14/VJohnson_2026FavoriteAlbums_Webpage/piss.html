My Review


PISS delivers a debut album unlike any other released this year with a heart-wrenching dialog on surviving sexual violence. A beautifully cohesive narrative from start to finish, meant to be experienced live but Taylor's haunting wails can echo throughout your heart regardless of where you consume it. Accompanied with samples of feminist author Andrea Dworkin, Toni Morrison, Mavis Nicholson, Manon Garcia, and Vikki Reynolds. Rape is a difficult thing to speak about in art as the language for discussing such topics is underdeveloped and overall 'frowned' upon in public parlance, however, Taylor delivers, through her screams and stumbling through emotion, a truthful and overwhelmingly haunting image of surviving the worst.

I have never heard a record quite like this. Most women are survivors, so I believe most of us will be able to relate to this album and the catharsis it can bring.


"Women are neither victims nor peaceful"


The most radicalizing and deeply felt spoken word segment comes from Andrea Dworkin, in 'andrea 1'

"And the major motif of pornography as a form of entertainment Is that women are raped and violated and humiliated until we discover that we like it And at that point, we ask for more"


It is rare for an album to leave me fundamentally changed on the other side, but you made me / skin on linen has done so. The anarcha-feminist essay Quiet Rumours is referenced, and is an important work on its own that all women desparate for freedom should read.
